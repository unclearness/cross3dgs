## The AGPL permits commercial use

The AGPL is not a noncommercial license. Anyone, including companies, may use
the software under it for any purpose, as long as they comply with its terms.
What those terms require depends on what you do with the software:

- You may make, run, and modify copies that you do not convey without further
  conditions, unless section 13 applies (AGPL section 2).
- If you convey a covered work (the software or a work based on it), you must
  comply with the AGPL's licensing and Corresponding Source requirements
  (sections 4 to 6). Separate and independent works that are merely
  aggregated with it on a storage or distribution medium are not
  automatically covered (section 5).
- If you modify the software and users interact with that modified version
  remotely through a computer network, section 13 requires that you
  prominently offer those users its Corresponding Source.
- Output from running the software, such as trained PLY files, images, and
  videos, is covered by the AGPL only if, given its content, it constitutes a
  covered work (section 2).

Research, evaluation, benchmarking, and academic publication need no
commercial license; if they involve conveying the software or serving a
modified version over a network, the conditions above apply to them as well.

## When a commercial license may be appropriate

A commercial license may be appropriate if you wish to:

- distribute a proprietary product or app that contains or is based on the
  software without meeting the AGPL's licensing and Corresponding Source
  requirements, or
- operate a modified version as a network service without offering its
  Corresponding Source to the users who interact with it.
